A Guidebook for the Wise and Ethical

Stewardshipof AI

“Thou shalt be diligent in preserving what thou hast, that thou mayest be a wise steward; for it is the free gift of the Lord thy God, and thou art his steward.”

D&C 136:27

Section One  ·  Purpose

As society continues to adapt to the ever-evolving technology of AI, it is important to remember that we are each stewards of our individual use of it and responsible for how it affects us, those around us, and the world we live in.

The purpose of this guidebook is not to give you a list of rules for how you should use AI, but rather to provide five principles that will sharpen the wise and ethical judgment you bring to it. Each principle is broken into three parts:

01 PrincipleA definition of what we are trying to safeguard.
02 PauseA question you can ask yourself when you’re uncertain about how to best use AI.
03 PracticeExamples of applying the principle and the pause. Each practice includes examples of AI misuse and AI wise use.

The Five Principles

01Agency

You are free to choose

Agency is the God-given gift to choose for yourself1 how to act and what to believe. AI can easily “diminish and suffocate [this] moral agency”2 when you allow it to make decisions or form opinions for you. Letting AI inform you is wise3. Letting it take the reins in your life is not. You are an agent to act and not to be acted upon4—not by any other person or technology. Cherish and protect your opportunities to use this gift.

“And because that they are redeemed from the fall they have become free forever, knowing good from evil; to act for themselves and not to be acted upon.”

2 Nephi 2:26

02Becoming

Refinement requires hardship

Becoming is a process of personal growth that extends far beyond any grade or diploma; it is a part of an education for eternity1. With AI, it has become easy to bypass this growth when we use it to bypass meaningful effort. Like any algorithm, it also treats errors as a flaw to be corrected. You are not an algorithm; your weaknesses2 and efforts can be a catalyst for profound change3. In situations where you can choose to offload your work or errors onto a machine, remember that the objective is not producing, presenting, or perfecting, but rather working and becoming who God intends for you to become4.

“Behold, I have refined thee, but not with silver; I have chosen thee in the furnace of affliction.”

Isaiah 48:10

“And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; and patience, experience; and experience, hope: and hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.”

Romans 5:3–5

03Fellowship

I am because we are

Fellowship is determined by intentional relationships with others. These relationships are at the core of your student experiences1 and are one of the strongest predictors of a happy and healthy life2. Technology, and now AI, can act as a frictionless supplement for human interactions3; it never judges or tires—but remember that it also never asks anything of you. People, who do judge and tire, can’t compete with the ease of turning to that technology, but their needs will grow you and your needs will grow them4. Be mindful of how you use AI and if your use of it is keeping you from strengthening your connections with those whom God has placed in your life.

04Discernment

Test the truth before you trust it

Discernment is the ability to distinguish truth from error, good from bad, and wise from foolish. Generative AI is making it increasingly difficult to mark these distinctions both in its unintentional hallucinations and in people using it to intentionally deceive others. Today we can still often tell what is AI and what isn’t1, but one day we may not be able to. If you can’t outright tell if something is real, wise, or good, pause to think through it, fact-check what you can, and listen to the spirit of truth2. You have been given a prophetic promise to discern truth from error3, so remain critical of the world’s “truths” and open to the spirit, and you will be blessed to see things as they really are4.

“For behold, my brethren, it is given unto you to judge, that ye may know good from evil; and the way to judge is as plain, that ye may know with a perfect knowledge, as the daylight is from the dark night.”

Mosiah 7:15

05Integrity

Be true at all times

Unfinished draft

Integrity is transparency, consistency, honesty, and admitting mistakes. When you make a commitment on how to use AI, stick to it. In difficulty, endure it. In uncertainty, ask. In error, confess. Be true to yourself at all times — in how you use it at school and in your personal life. No one is looking over your shoulder to make sure you are using it right, and no one will be grading or judging how you use it 100% of the time. It is up to you. Examine how you use AI. Do you show integrity?

“And they were all young men, and they were exceedingly valiant for courage, and also for strength and activity; but behold, this was not all—they were men who were true at all times in whatsoever thing they were entrusted. Yea, they were men of truth and soberness, for they had been taught to keep the commandments of God and to walk uprightly before him.”

Alma 53:20–21
